What is Watermarking

  • Watermarking is the process of **embedding a visible or invisible mark or identifier** into **a digital image, video, document, or any other type of media**.

  • The purpose of watermarking is to **establish ownership** or **provide copyright protection** to the creator of the content.

  • Watermarks can also be used for branding, ensuring authenticity, and deterring unauthorized use or distribution of the media

  • Watermarks can take different forms, depending on the desired visibility and purpose.

  • They can be as simple as a text overlay with the creator’s name or a logo, or they can be more complex, such as embedding a unique identifier within the pixels of an image or altering the image’s characteristics slightly to create a distinguishable pattern.

Type

  1. Visible Watermarks

    1. These watermarks are intentionally designed to be visible and can be easily recognized on the media

    2. They are often placed in a corner or overlaid throughout the content and typically contain text, logos, or graphical elements.

    3. Visible watermarks are commonly used for branding purposes, such as displaying a company’s logo on their promotional images or videos.

  2. Invisible Watermarks

    1. Also known as digital watermarks or steganographic watermarks, these marks are embedded within the media but are not immediately visible to the naked eye.

    2. Invisible watermarks are designed to be difficult to remove or alter without degrading the quality of the content.

    3. They can be used to prove ownership or track unauthorized distribution by containing unique information about the creator or the content itself.

Technique

  • Watermarking techniques can vary depending on the type of media and the desired level of protection.

  • Some common methods used for watermarking include

Image-based watermarks

  • Adding a visible or invisible watermark directly onto an image

Audio-based watermarks

  • Embedding watermarks within audio files using imperceptible modifications

Video-based watermarks

  • Adding watermarks to video content, either as visible overlays or as invisible marks embedded within the video frames

Document-based watermarks

  • Inserting watermarks into documents such aas PDFs, ensuring that they are visible when the document is viewed or printed

  • Watermarking can be an effective way to deter unauthorized use and protect important to note that determined individuals may still find ways to remove or tamper with watermarks, so it should not be considered a foolproof method of protection.

Answers

NexGuard

  • “For technical there are limited documentations for the MediaConvert integration may I know if there is any more details documentation and technical materials?” I don’t think you will get any more details apart from the explanation found in the ‘info’ of the NexGuard File Marker section in the MediaConvert Job ‘Encoding Settings’. Any further details on the NexGuard File Marker will need to come from Nagra Presales team who can provide more details and then advise how to configure the settings as well share the process to get the required license. I would advise you to get your customer to submit an inquiry via this page (https://dtv.nagra.com/contact-us) to get in touch with Nagra.

  • On the NexGuard license ID, this is something Nagra will likely need to generate per customer and not something you can self-service. The reason is there are other components in the watermarking solution which the customer will need which Nagra will offer as well: watermark retrieval service.

  • I found this blog post in collaboration with Nagra’s Product Director which will give you a bit more insights to their watermarking
    solutions: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/media/protecting-pre-and-early-release-content-with-nagra-nexguard-forensic-watermarking/

  • Additional information to share my understanding and experience: Watermarking is a security feature to embed imperceptible marks which is meant to prevent piracy and also to provide some traceability to who is responsible on the piracy of the content. As such, how the content is watermarked is a closely guarded intellectual property. This is why you will not find specific technical details on this anywhere. Each watermark technology provider such as Nagra, Verimatrix, Synamedia, Irdeto all have their own proprietary method of implementing the watermark.

  • Also, watermarking solution has 2 parts to it.

    • First part is to embed the watermark and in this case, it is at transcoding time at the transcoder. Again there are 2 different ways to
      do it for different use cases.
      a. For Pre-Release content, e.g. distribution to different distributor, etc, payload ID is used to be embedded into the video. So for different distributor, you will use different payload ID to distinguish them. So when a video is leaked, you will be able to tell from which distributor is the content leaked from.

    • b. For OTT streaming, it uses a different approach. It uses an approach called Server-side session-based watermarking. What this means at a high level is that you need 2 streams for each content. I’ll call it Content1-A and content1-B. Content1-A will be marked with a symbol, number or something but is the same for this content. Content1-B is marked with another symbol, number or something but is the same for this content. When a user stream this video (which is made up of Content1-A and Content1-B streams), the CDN will dynamically generate the playlist in such a way that it is alternating between Content1-A and Content1-B
      in different permutation for different segments of the video such that it forms a unique pattern (to signal the session ID) for the particular streaming session for different users. The CDN in this case plays an important role as well as it needs to have the intelligence/capability to dynamically provide unique playlist per user per stream per session.

  • Second part is the watermark retrieval from leaked sources to retrieve back the payload ID or session ID. This is something that Watermark solution provider will provide and managed. This will be something your customer will need to enter into a commercial agreement. So, the cost to your customer is not just embedding the watermark via MediaConvert, but also need to consider this watermark retrieval of leaked content. Even though you will not do this processing for all content, only those leaked ones, it is still a cost to consider.

Invisible Watermark

  • The functionality you’re referring to is often dubbed forensic watermarking as opposed to a graphical overlay or watermark. There are a few techniques for implementing this, but only a handful of commercial options. AWS Elemental has worked with Civolution/Nexguard to integrate their forensic watermarking solution into Elemental Cloud, however, this is not available in AWS Elemental MediaConvert or MediaPackage (the console services) today. If the customer would like to see support of a specific forensic system integrated into our managed encoding/packaging services, I’d recommend opening a PFR.

  • In the meantime, the customer should reach out directly to the companies that provide the watermarking services like Nexguard and Castlabs as they’ll have recommended approaches for deploying their technology on AWS.
