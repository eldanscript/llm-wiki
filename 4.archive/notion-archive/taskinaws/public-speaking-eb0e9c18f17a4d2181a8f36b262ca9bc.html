Public Speaking

Practice

  • Practice in front of a mirror

  • Record yourself on camera and watch the recording

  • Practice in front of friends or colleagues

  • Take all the feedback and make adjustments, add customer stories, anecdotes, and humor

  • Rehearsal checklist

    • Body Language

      • Pacing, eye contact, hand gestures, and anything distracting

    • Tone

      • Can you understand the speaker clearly?

      • Look for filler words, pauses, excessive repeating, and unknown acronyms

    • Deck

      • Are the slides and visuals clear to the audience and easy to read

    • Content

      • Is the topic and storyline clear?

      • Is the presentation easy to follow along for an audience?

      • Make sure the content visually follows the topic you are discussing

Knowing audience

  • This is for the customer

    • Content is something the audience or customer is excited about and stay on message

  • Your audience will guide your message

    • Include anecdotes, humor, technical depth, customer example, and more

  • Connect with the audience while speaking

    • Dividing the audience into segments that you can make eye contact with, smile at, and gesture toward

Presentation structure

  • Grabber

    • Customer quote

    • Customer example

    • Statistic

    • Personal story

  • Middle

    • Stay on topic

    • Be clear

    • Go slow

    • Raise and lower volume

    • Do not use acronyms

    • Do not have a monotone voice

    • Do not over simplify

  • Close

    • Summarize

    • Strong Statement

    • Takeaway / Action Item

Telling stories

  • Customer stories

  • AWS story

  • Product stories

Using effective language

  • Concrete Language

    • Facts, Strong, succinct statements

  • Indecisive Language

    • Kind of, I guess

  • Identify inclusive language

Pauses, gestures and awkward fillers

  • Pauses help you gather your thoughts as well as slow down your presentation

  • Gestures should be used to effectively engage with the audience and relay your message

  • Awkward fillers like ‘uh’, ‘um’ or ‘like’ usually get used when presenters are nervous and not well-practiced. avoid as many fillers words as possible

Storytelling

What makes a great story

  • Personal

  • Vulnerability and authenticity

  • Sense of conflict

  • Simple

  • Delivery

A great story is about everyone, or it will not last

How do I tell a story

  • Typical narrative structure

    • Setup

      • Introduction of characters and setting

    • Conflict

      • Introduction to the problem

    • Rising Action

      • Plot builds toward the climax

    • Climax

      • The major turning point in the story, often the most suspenseful moment

    • Falling Action

      • Major events leading to the resolution

    • Resolution

      • Conflict resolved and benefits summarized

Customer storytelling

  • Dive deep

    • Know your customer’s business

      • What’s their mission? Vision? History? Industry?

    • Know the visual identity of the customer

      • What is their brand? Voice?

  • Earn trust

    • Build rapport with the speaker

    • Sense of trust with the customer

    • Face-to-face

  • Building a story

    • Begging

      • Explain the impact, Describe the business challenge

    • Middle

      • Explain why we choose AWS, Describe the technical challenge, Describe the plan

    • End

      • Explain how we’re looking ahead, Describe technical & business outcomes achieved

    • Tricks & Tips

      • Reflection - What do you think the audience will think of this?

      • Ask them to record themselves

      • Send them keynotes or content you think are great

      • Redirect them to the theme or takeaways

      • Remind them of their limited time

Amazon voice

  • Simple

    • We delight the customer by keeping everything reliable and easy-to-use

    • Simple is not cold, mechanical, plain

  • Empowering

    • We’re constantly inventing on behalf of the customer to reduce friction and make life more simple

    • Empowering is not self-absorbed, bragging, exaggerated

  • Welcoming

    • Every customer everywhere should feel like Amazon is created just for them

    • Welcoming is not irreverent, pretentious, overfamiliar

  • Optimistic

    • We emphasize solutions, expansion, and the fun of continuous discovery

    • Optimistic is not bubbly, over-excited, unrealistic

  • Authentic

    • We stay grounded in the stories, words, and needs of actual customers

    • Authentic is not unprofessional, humorless, unpolished

  • Sound like people talking to people - Sound more like a person and less like a corporation

  • Voice is professional but conversational - Refer to ourselves as ‘we’ and our customers as ‘you’

  • Use the simplest language possible - Explain our complex products and features as clearly as we can without leaning on opaque jargon or marketing speak

Stage Presence

Stage presence

  • The ability of a speaker to capture and command the attention of an audience

First impressions

  • Walking out on stage with confidence

  • Your grabber

  • Planning your anchor point ahead

  • Walk with a purpose

  • Enter the stage with a smile

Confidence

  • Project the most idyllic version of yourself

  • Use great posture

  • Know you are the subject matter expert

  • Don’t criticize your own mistakes while on stage

Powerful stances

  • Expanding, Stretch out, Take up space

  • Open up, Stand in the middle, Spread out, Raise hands high

Anxiety and nervousness

  • Know your content

  • Practice

  • Pause

  • Visualize your success

  • No one is perfect

  • Take deep breaths

Stage positioning

  • Back : Big picture information

  • Middle : Customer stories and anecdotes

  • Front : Main content

  • Very Front : Calling the audience to action

  • Left/Right Side : Important technical slides

  • Center : Majority of your content

  • Body position on stage to three sections

Body language

  • Be natural

  • Posture

  • Smile

  • Move

  • Hands

  • Hands gestures importance

    • The first place people look is the hands

    • Show speakers are experts in their concepts

    • Speakers can show concept on two tracks, words and hands

Speaking voice

  • Don’t talk too fast

  • Show your pace and use pauses

  • Raise voice : call to actions, exciting facts

  • Lower voice : main content

Sessions and audience engagement

Breakout sessions

  • Length : 30 ~ 60 mins

  • Style : Lecture

  • Audience size : Usually 100+

  • QnA : last 10 mins

  • Deck : Yes

  • Downstage monitor (DSM) : depends on event

  • Speaker certification : required

Live silent sessions

  • Length : 30 ~ 60 mins

  • Style : Lecture

  • Audience size : Usually 100+

  • QnA : No live QnA

  • Deck : Yes

  • Downstage monitor (DSM) : depends on event

  • Speaker certification : required

  • Occur simultaneously within the same room

  • Screens are highlighted in different colors and headphones that light up in matching colors are placed on each of the seats. The audience selects a seat with headphones that match the presentation they wish to attend

Chalk Talks

  • Length : 60 mins, interactive

  • Style : Classroom style

  • Audience size : 50 ~ 70

  • QnA : Yes, throughout

  • Deck : Limited

  • Downstage monitor (DSM) : depend on event

  • Speaker certification : No, unless the session is recorded

Builders sessions

  • Length : 60 mins, interactive

  • Style : Single table, hands-on, laptops out

  • Audience size : 6 ~ 9

  • QnA : Yes, throughout

  • Deck : Limited

  • Downstage monitor (DSM) : No

  • Speaker certification : No, unless the session is recorded

Workshops

  • Length : 120 mins, interactive

  • Style : Hands-on, laptops out

  • Audience size : 100+

  • QnA : Yes, throughout

  • Deck : Limited

  • Downstage monitor (DSM) : depends on event

  • Speaker certification : Yes

Customer meetings

  • Length : Varies

  • Style : Meeting room

  • Audience size : Usually 1:1

  • QnA : Yes, throughout

  • Deck : Depends

  • Downstage monitor (DSM) : No

  • Speaker certification : No

Speaker certification

  • Breakout session, live silent session, workshop

  • Audience size : 100+

  • Recording : whenever content is recorded

  • Press : whenever there is press present in the audience

  • QnA : 100+

  • Webinars : whenever you a present material on a digital platform

Audience engagement tips

  • Average adult attention span has plummeted from 12 mins a decade ago to just 5 mins now

  • Ask the audience : ask the audience members 2~3 questions about what they’d like to learn

    • What do you want to learn today?

    • What do you want out of this presentation?

  • Raise your hands : to better understand who they are or just have fun

    • Raise your hand if you just came here for the free food

    • Raise your hand if this is your first time at re:Invent

  • Keep a good story going … : start an interesting story, but wait to finish it until the end

    • I just want to tell you a story that is very important for my talk today…

    • I’ll get back to that story towards the end

  • Make the room smaller : break the ice among your audience

    • Tell the audience to say hello to the people next them or say a phrase…

  • Involve the audience

    • Ask the audience for help with moving the presentation along

    • What AWS services stand out to you most in this architecture?

    • Why do you think we structured it this way?

  • Pop Quiz

  • Poll the audience

Live silent session engagement

  • No mic, no problem

    • Ask the audience to clap or stand in response to questions

    • Clap if you’re a software developer

  • Start a friendly competition

    • If you know fellow speakers, see how many headsets you can turn to the color of your stage.

    • Use the colors to your advantage to track your own audience engagement

  • QnA

    • Ask your audience to meet you off stage or at an AWS booth to answer any questions

Speaker Scores

What are speaker scores

  • Customer ratings on the effectiveness of a speaker

  • CSAT score vs Speaker effectiveness

    • CSAT Question : Overall how satisfied were you with this session?

    • Speaker Effectiveness : How effective was the speaker in presenting the content?

    • Anecdotal Feedback Question : Did you learn something new in this session?, Open feedback

Where are speaker scores collected

  • AWS re:Invent, AWS Summit, AWS RE:INFORCE

Why are speaker scores important

  • Customer Obsession

  • Data Driven

  • Insist on the Highest Standards

When will scores start being tracked

  • Fall 2020

Speaker Bar Raiser program overview

  • Bar Raisers

    • Understand AWS messaging

    • Perform well on stage

    • Have a passion for public speaking

    • Spoken on 5+ events

    • Average score of 4.8+

  • Bar Raisers will

    • Teach and mentor

    • Promote public speaking best practices

    • Uphold AWS messaging and Amazon voice

    • Advocate for all speaker programs

  • Certification revoked

    • Speaker Development Mentorship (SDM)

      • Speakers who meet the 3 below 4.0 rule : scores below 4.0 for three

      • do not enter and complete the speaker development mentorship program within 3 months

    • Case by case basis decided by the Speaker Certification team

      • if a speaker violates AWS PR guidelines

      • Speaking openly about competitors on stage

      • Referencing NDAs publically

      • Naming confidential customers
