[Stock broadcast summary PoC] AWS has been unsuccessful in being selected as the technical partner for Hankyung TV’s project to summarize its stock recommendation broadcasts using generative AI. The initiative aimed to transcribe and summarize 24/7 broadcasts for end customers, delivering keyword-based messages to attract users to their platform. The project’s success hinged on the ability to provide accurate and rapid summaries. A total of three vendors (AWS, GCP, Naver) participated in the PoC. In the initial stages, the customer had doubts about the possibility of segmenting the broadcast content based on context and generating text summaries for each segmented video. The account team demonstrated the feasibility to the customer by providing a summary of a 30-minute sample video using Amazon Transcribe and Anthropic Claude3.5 Sonnet. Based on this feasibility, the customer decided to proceed with a PoC for generating summaries of full-day stock broadcast. The account team was collaborating with GSN for the PoC. The account team supported the GSN’s development, enhancing the context-based video segment classification by applying advanced prompting techniques and refine-based context analysis methods. Additionally, the account team improved the quality of Korean scripts and summaries by implementing LLM-based typo correction to reduce the errors of Korean transcript generated by Amazon Transcribe. However, the evaluation shifted focus toward the accuracy of the full transcribed text, leading to the selection of Naver, which achieved 100% accuracy with human resource involvement. The key factor leading to these results was that the evaluation of the PoC was led not by the IT department, but by a group composed of broadcast journalists. As a result, the accuracy of script conversion became the key decision criterion, rather than automation and cost-effective implementation.

Basic IVS Inquiries

1. Is the IVS SDK required for both broadcasting and viewing?

Answer:

For broadcasting (RTMPs)

Any software that can broadcast RTMP(s), such as OBS, can be used.

For viewing (HLS):

Any software that can play HLS (Video.js, SW Player, ExoPlayer, AVPlayer etc) can be used. However, we highly recommend using the IVS SDKs to ensure best quality and lowest latency. With IVS SDK, IVS team can also provide best support to catch errors and improve product quality.

For RTX (WebRTC):

for publishing (sending): you can use OBS (Whip) or any other RTMPS streaming software

for subscribing (viewing): you need to use IVS Broadcast SDK

2. After the live stream ends, can the replay video be obtained as a single file such as an mp4?

Answer:

Both LL / RT: replay video is available but it’s available as a form of HLS (with ABR option). you can convert that to mp4 using a public software such as MediaConverter, ffmpeg

1. How can subtitles be inputted?

Answer:

LL product:

IVS supports captions in the industry standard CEA-708/EIA-608 format

(reference)

. OBS Plugins for LL transcription are available which publish in the standard format.

However, IVS does not support double byte captions and multi-track captions playback.

In case of double byte caption and multi-track(multi-language) captions, IVS recommend using out of band caption (using IVS timed metadata or a separate websocket connection).

RT product:

captions and/or translations must be managed manually and can be published via WebSocket connection or any other publishing protocol.

2. What is the delay time due to the addition of subtitles?

Answer:

Depends on the method chosen. Timed metadata has latency of 3-4 second in average.

Answer:

if you use current caption support (608,708), we can only do latin language and one language at a time today

if you use timed-metadata, the only limit is “5 requests per second per channel are allowed, each with a maximum 1 KB payload” (

reference

). the TPS can be increased
