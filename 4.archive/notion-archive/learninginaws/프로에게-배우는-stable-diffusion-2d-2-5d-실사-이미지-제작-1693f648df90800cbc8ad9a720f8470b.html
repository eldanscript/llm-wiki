  • VAE (Variational Autoencoder)

    • 미세 detail 조정, input/output 담당

    • Detail explanation

      In the context of Stable Diffusion models, a VAE (Variational Autoencoder) is a key component used to learn a compressed representation of data. Stable Diffusion, a type of generative model capable of creating detailed images from textual descriptions, utilizes various components in its architecture to understand and generate visual content effectively. One of these components is the VAE.

      Variational Autoencoder (VAE) Explained:

      A Variational Autoencoder is a type of neural network that aims to learn a latent (hidden) representation of input data. It has two main parts: an encoder and a decoder. The encoder compresses the input data into a latent space (a compact representation), and the decoder reconstructs the input data from this compact representation. VAEs are particularly powerful in generative tasks because they can learn to model the distribution of input data in an unsupervised manner.

      Role of VAE in Stable Diffusion:

      In the case of Stable Diffusion models:

      • Latent Space Representation: The VAE learns to encode images into a lower-dimensional latent space and then decode these latent representations back into images. This process effectively captures the underlying structure of the image data.

      • Generation Process: When generating new images, the model can manipulate the latent space representations to produce variations of the decoded images, allowing for the generation of new content that shares similarities with the training data but is not a direct copy.

      • Control and Flexibility: By understanding the latent space, Stable Diffusion models can be guided to generate images that follow specific instructions or meet certain criteria, enhancing the model’s ability to produce relevant and diverse outputs based on textual prompts.

      The use of a VAE within Stable Diffusion is an example of how generative models leverage advanced neural network architectures to understand and create complex data representations. This approach allows Stable Diffusion models to generate high-quality, diverse images from textual descriptions, making them highly effective for tasks requiring creative image generation.

    • Detail Explanation

      As of my last update in April 2023, the reference to “ViT-L/14” in the context of Stable Diffusion and models like CLIP (Contrastive Language–Image Pre-training) relates to the architecture used for understanding and generating visual content based on textual descriptions.

      ViT-L/14 Explained:

      • ViT-L/14 stands for Vision Transformer-Large with a 14x14 patch size. The Vision Transformer (ViT) is a type of neural network that applies the transformer architecture, originally designed for natural language processing (NLP), to image recognition tasks. The “Large” denotes a larger size or capacity of the model, with more layers and parameters compared to its smaller counterparts. The “14” refers to the resolution of the patches the image is divided into for processing; in this case, each patch is 14x14 pixels.

      CLIP and its Role:

      • CLIP (Contrastive Language–Image Pre-training) is a model developed by OpenAI that learns visual concepts from natural language supervision. It’s trained on a variety of images and text from the internet, allowing it to understand and generate content across a broad range of styles and subjects. CLIP models can efficiently associate images with relevant textual descriptions, making them powerful tools for tasks that involve interpreting or generating images based on text.

      Integration in Stable Diffusion:

      • In the Stable Diffusion model, components like CLIP can be used to guide the image generation process, helping the model to better align the generated images with the textual prompts provided by the user. This makes the model highly versatile and capable of producing images that closely match the user’s intentions.

While ViT-L/14 and CLIP have been foundational, the field of AI and machine learning is rapidly advancing, with continuous improvements and updates to model architectures to enhance performance, efficiency, and the quality of generated content.

  • U-Net

    • Image 생성을 위한 DL model

    • Detail Explanation

      U-Net is a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture that was originally designed for biomedical image segmentation. It was introduced by Olaf Ronneberger, Philipp Fischer, and Thomas Brox in their paper “U-Net: Convolutional Networks for Biomedical Image Segmentation,” presented at the MICCAI 2015 conference. The architecture is particularly notable for its effectiveness in tasks where the input images and their corresponding output segmentation maps are closely aligned, allowing for precise localization and segmentation of objects within images, even with a relatively small amount of training data.

      Key Features of U-Net:

      • Architecture: U-Net has a distinctive U-shaped architecture, consisting of a contracting path (encoder) to capture context and a symmetric expanding path (decoder) for precise localization. This structure enables the model to capture both high-level feature information and fine-grained details simultaneously.

      • Skip Connections: The model includes skip connections between the corresponding layers in the encoder and decoder paths. These connections concatenate feature maps from the encoder to the decoder, helping to preserve spatial information that is often lost in deep convolutional networks. This feature is crucial for tasks that require detailed pixel-level predictions, such as image segmentation.

      • Efficiency: U-Net is designed to work with a limited amount of training data and still produce high-quality results. This efficiency makes it especially valuable in medical imaging, where annotated images can be scarce and expensive to produce.

      Applications of U-Net:

      While U-Net was originally developed for biomedical image segmentation, such as segmenting cells or tissues in microscopic images, its application has expanded significantly. It is now used in a wide range of image segmentation tasks beyond the medical field, including satellite image analysis, object detection in autonomous vehicles, and even in art and generative models where precise segmentation is required.

      The design principles of U-Net, particularly its ability to combine low-level detail with high-level contextual information, have influenced the development of numerous other neural network architectures aimed at efficient and accurate image segmentation.

03-01. Model

  • Diffusion Model

    • Diffusion Process Forward

      • 이미지 x0로부터 모든 픽셀값에 시간 t마다 Noise를 첨가해주는 Process

      • Image의 Pixel 값에 정규 분포를 따르는 Noise 값을 추가 → 이 과정을 매우 많이 반복 → 많은 시간 t를 거쳐 최종적으로 완전 균등한 상태로 분포

    • Reverse Diffusion Process

      • 시간 t 마다 이미지 픽셀값에 첨가된 Noise 값을 계산할 수 있다면 Noise 이미지 xT 로부터 진짜 이미지 x0로 되돌리는 것이 가능 → 특정 단계의 이미지 생성 가능
